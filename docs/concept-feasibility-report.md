# Measure the Delta's own rotation experiments

The strongest Field Shift entry for a Cairo team is a hybrid none of the five candidates gets to alone. Call it a **"Precrop Payoff Atlas"**. NASA's Prithvi-EO-2.0, run on HLS, rebuilds the Nile Delta's crop sequences. A causal-forest engine (candidate 2) then measures what each observed rotation did to the next crop's vigor and water use. Analog-field retrieval shows each farmer "fields like yours that already switched", and the salinity thresholds and the rice decree (candidate 4) act as hard constraints. A tool-restricted Egyptian-Arabic agent presents the result. Candidate 2 is the most defensible core: its method has peer-reviewed precedent from the Lobell group, it has never been applied to an irrigated smallholder system like the Delta, it fits the Kaggle budget easily, and its per-option effect-with-uncertainty output matches the brief's demand for evidence, gaps and risks. The Rotation World Model (1) is novel as a framing, but on observational satellite data it learns "what fields that chose X looked like", not "what X does". It survives only as a small one-season forecaster checked against the causal estimates. EMIT soil carbon (3) is the weakest: no published EMIT SOC study exists, Delta SOC varies little, and nothing local can validate it. Salinity (4) and canal-level planning (5) are better as layers than as headlines. This matters because the 2026 field is crowded with NASA POWER, weighted-score and chatbot entries, including at least one Egyptian team already shipping ECOSTRESS-based salinity diagnosis with an Egyptian-Arabic advisor. Only a concept that turns satellite history into *measured, falsifiable evidence* stands apart on Validity and Creativity together.

## The brief demands evidence and uncertainty for three or more rotations, not just a ranking

Participant repos reproduce the official challenge text word for word. It asks teams to "compare at least three rotation strategies, including the existing rotation". For each one the tool should "explain why it was suggested, what evidence supports it, what benefits it may offer, where information gaps exist, and any risks that would need local review" ([fieldwitness official_challenge.md](https://github.com/amr-mousa-eg/fieldwitness/blob/main/docs/official_challenge.md)). The suggested datasets are **SMAP, ECOSTRESS, GPM, Landsat, MODIS, NASA POWER, GRACE/GRACE-FO and SRTM**. The modeling section asks teams to "explain how the model interprets uncertainty and where local expertise would be needed". It also asks them to harmonize SMAP, ECOSTRESS, GPM, Landsat/MODIS indices, GEDI biomass and soils onto a common grid ([same](https://github.com/amr-mousa-eg/fieldwitness/blob/main/docs/official_challenge.md)). The tool is framed as supporting farmers, not replacing them. Farmer priorities listed include water use, forage value, nitrogen fixing, erosion, organic matter and market suitability. A local organizer's copy of the Resources adds VIIRS, NLDAS/GLDAS, OpenET, FAO GAEZ, Sentinel-2 and ERA5. The rest of that list is US-centric (SSURGO, CDL, NASS) ([HackAthlone](https://hackathlone.com/challenges/adapting-farms-with-nasa-data)). A Delta team must therefore name its own substitutes, and doing so explicitly earns Validity credit.

Two procedural points remain unverified. The indexed 2026 FAQ reportedly allows AI tools only if they are disclosed in a mandatory submission question, and non-NASA materials must be openly licensed ([SOURCES.md](https://github.com/senitheknath/nasa-app-challege/blob/main/preparation/SOURCES.md)). Official judging weights and video limits were not confirmed, because the Space Apps site failed TLS during research. The five-equal-criteria assumption therefore comes from your brief, not from a checked rubric. The official Resources tab was due 13 Nov and should be re-checked when the event opens.

### Over forty 2026 entries crowd the obvious design space

GitHub shows **more than 40 "field shift" repos** from this cycle. Most feed NASA POWER (sometimes with SMAP or MODIS) into a rule-based or weighted-score ranker, then add a bilingual UI or chat advisor. Three competitors matter most for a Cairo team. **BlinkFarms** targets Egyptian farmers with ECOSTRESS LST/ESI, SMAP L4, GPM and POWER, FAO-56 and TOPSIS ranking, a "field memory" state-space model, subsoil salinity and hardpan diagnosis, and a Gemini advisor "fluent in Egyptian Arabic dialect" ([BlinkFarms](https://github.com/umar-essayed/BlinkFarms-NASA-SpaceApps)). **FieldWitness**, apparently by an Egyptian author but deployed on a Kansas HLS tile, offers an HLS field history since 2015, A–D evidence grades, "smallest change that would flip the top option", falsifiable next-season predictions and a hindcast scoreboard ([fieldwitness](https://github.com/amr-mousa-eg/fieldwitness)). **FIELDSHIFT AI** is bilingual Arabic/English on POWER plus SMAP ([repo](https://github.com/a7mdel5taeeb-eng/fieldshift-ai)). Elsewhere, teams have taken historical-year replay ("Rotation Time Machine"), analog climate years, growing-window shift and parametric-insurance linkage ([ferrosasfp](https://github.com/ferrosasfp/field-shift)).

That leaves clear gaps. Nobody surveyed uses Prithvi, crop-sequence reconstruction or causal estimation of rotation effects, and nobody uses NISAR. BlinkFarms already holds Egypt-targeting, salinity and the Egyptian-Arabic LLM. FieldWitness already holds falsifiable predictions and evidence grades. Precedent helps the framing: Egypt's Zumorroda-X won a 2025 global award in an agriculture challenge, though as an educational game ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/stem-engagement-at-nasa/nasa-announces-2025-international-space-apps-challenge-global-winners/)). NASA's own 2026 Space-to-Soil challenge recognized a Prithvi-distillation agriculture concept, which signals institutional appetite for Prithvi-plus-agriculture ([ESTO](https://esto.nasa.gov/?p=5934)). In the Delta itself, the only deployed tool found is FutureWater's Croptimal, a WhatsApp crop-resilience app in Sharkia, and its rotation work lives in demo plots rather than the app ([FutureWater BUCRA](https://www.futurewater.eu/projects/bucra/)).

**Candidate 2** stands on firm ground. Kluger, Di Tommaso and Lobell used causal forests on satellite yield proxies (calibrated peak GCVI) across the US, Canada, France and NE China. They found rotation "generally" helps, spring wheat benefits most, and moving beyond two crops adds little: the two-crop benefit is roughly **3.7× the extra diversification benefit** ([arXiv 2506.02384](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02384v2)). Deines et al. ran the same toolkit on more than 90,000 fields for cover crops and on Landsat tillage maps ([PNNL](https://data.pnnl.gov/group/nodes/publication/33662); [ERL 2019](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ab503b/meta)). No satellite causal study of rotations in Egypt or any North African irrigated system turned up, and none in any smallholder system. That is the sharp angle: a first precrop-payoff estimate for berseem/wheat → rice/maize/cotton. There is a plausible signal to find. Egyptian trials repeatedly name berseem the best preceding crop: rice after berseem at Tag El-Ezz in Dakahlia had the highest means for all traits, and wheat after clover won at Assiut ([EKB Dakahlia](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_257186_9c13162afbfa131f3df085f05509a5d2.pdf); [EKB Assiut](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_36384_16cfcbe3193150ca9923afb030a5fb14.pdf)). The method's own caveats will become the judges' questions: unmeasured fertilizer, map misclassification, one-year precrop effects only, spatially correlated errors and proxy outcomes. Kluger 2022 states plainly that satellite-only estimates are not reliable causal effects without trial anchoring ([arXiv 2112.13700](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.13700)). Signals can also vanish, as one Flanders study found ([OUCI](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4KeOzAJ9)).

**Candidate 1** is novel in framing, and that novelty hides a structural flaw. The leading satellite vegetation world models are GreenEarthNet/Contextformer, VegeDiff and the June 2026 EO-WM video-diffusion model. All of them condition on weather only, and none conditions on management or plans ([arXiv 2303.16198](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.16198v2); [arXiv 2606.27277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27277)). Rotation RL exists, but inside process simulators: CyclesGym is the only multi-year crop-planning benchmark ([NeurIPS 2022](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2022/poster/55732)), and MILP rotation planning dates back at least to 2011 ([Dury et al. review](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00930508)). So MCTS/CEM planning brings no new idea; only a satellite-learned dynamics model would. That dynamics model would be trained on actions the farmer chose, and those choices follow location, salinity and the rice-zone decree. A 2026 review of agricultural world models warns of exactly this "schedule memory trap": the model memorizes management calendars instead of learning action effects ([Zenodo 21699171](https://zenodo.org/records/21699171)). A planner then hunts out the model's errors in sequences that were rare in the data, the familiar offline model-based RL failure (inference). Crop emulators show the same out-of-distribution collapse: R² above 0.95 at training sites fell apart at unseen locations ([Johnston 2023](https://eld.bl.uk/catalog/100180515115_0x000001)). With roughly 8–10 seasons of reconstructable history, a "5–10-year soil-health trajectory" is out of reach. What survives is a crop-conditional next-season vigor/ET forecaster, compared against persistence and weather-only baselines and checked against the causal-forest estimates.

**Candidate 4** frames a real problem. Commonly cited figures put **30–40% of Delta soils as salt-affected** and **37.7% of the Delta in the high seawater-intrusion vulnerability class**, though these use different baselines ([Brunel review](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/29876/1/FullText.pdf)). Rice is the north's leaching crop, yet Decree 26/2025 capped it at **724,200 feddans** in designated governorates, with delivery from 15 May to 31 Aug ([Elwatan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/7820108)). The decree lets you write a policy-aware rule engine, with Maas–Hoffman thresholds such as rice 3.0, wheat 6.0, sugar beet 7.0 and barley 8.0 dS/m ([LibreTexts](https://workforce.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Water_Systems_Technology/Irrigation_Systems_Management_(AET_021)/07%3A_Salinity_Management/7.04%3A_Crop_Salt_Tolerance)). But BlinkFarms already pitches salinity for Egypt, and Egyptian S1/S2 ML salinity mapping is a crowded literature. Its high R² values (GBM 0.89) come from small dryland calibration sets ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ea871f74b6ce4fbd8d5dbc3a82bcb5dd)), and a 2025 Delta ensemble reportedly reached only R² 0.396 against an index target. Per-field *forecasting* is not credible with NASA data. GRACE-FO works at roughly basin scale, and the 1 km downscaled groundwater product ends in 2020 ([ESSD 2026](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/7593/2026/)). The 1 km SMAP/Sentinel-1 product "has not [been validated]" ([NSIDC](https://nsidc.org/data/spl2smap_s)). Salinity belongs in the hybrid as risk mapping plus legal and agronomic constraints, not as a forecast.

**Candidate 5** rests on real institutions: rotational canal turns, documented head/tail inequity with rice clustering where water is plentiful ([IWMI](https://iwmi.org/news/heads-or-tails)), mesqa WUAs, and a smart-card registry being matched to satellite coordinates. No paper combines LLM farmer agents with a hard canal-water solver, so it is novel. Three weaknesses hold it back. No public shapefile of branch-canal or mesqa command areas exists, only proxies such as GRAIN ([ESSD 2026](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/1855/2026/)). GovSim shows LLM agents sustained a shared resource in only **2 of 45 runs** ([arXiv 2404.16698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16698)), so a solver must enforce water limits, and judges will then ask what the LLM adds. Coordinated rotation also echoes the mandatory rotation Egypt abolished in 1987 ([FAO Y4632E](https://www.fao.org/3/Y4632E/y4632e0c.htm)). It works as a single-canal "what if neighbours coordinated" demo slide, and not as the core.

**Candidate 3** has coverage and nothing to validate against. EMIT is in extended operations "through at least 2026" ([Earthdata](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/data/instruments/emit-imaging-spectrometer)). A first-hand CMR catalogue query during this research counted about 100 L2A acquisition dates over the Delta from Aug 2022 to Aug 2026, including the April–May and October bare-soil windows. Forward processing of V001/V002 stopped on 3 Sep 2026 for reprocessing ([Earthdata alert](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/emit-version-1-existing-version-2-forward-processing-ended)). The only official soil-relevant product is L2B mineral identification (calcite, kaolinite, montmorillonite, gypsum and others) at 60 m ([EMITL2BMIN](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/lpcloud-emitl2bmin-002)). No peer-reviewed EMIT SOC or clay paper exists. Spaceborne hyperspectral SOC elsewhere manages roughly R² 0.55 / RPD 1.5 on real PRISMA ([EGU26-13611](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-13611.html)). A 60 m pixel is larger than a typical Delta plot, Egyptian soil reference data are thin, and a Wallonia study of 34,418 parcels found satellite SOC's minimum detectable difference *exceeded* the decade-scale change carbon farming produces ([EGU26-17841](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-17841.html)). That last result rules out "carbon-building rotations" as a measurable claim from orbit.

## Better ideas exist, and the best ones attach to the causal engine

The scouting surfaced five angles absent from the candidate list. **Prithvi-EO-2.0 crop-sequence reconstruction** is the most valuable. It uses NASA's own foundation model, no competitor uses it, and it produces the "existing rotation" baseline the brief requires. **Analog-field retrieval** has precedent: embeddings have been shown to capture multi-year rotation dynamics ([ISPRS Annals 2026](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/XI-2-2026/865/2026/isprs-annals-XI-2-2026-865-2026.xml)), but nobody has used them to retrieve evidence for rotation advice. It is essentially the matching step of candidate 2 made visible to farmers, which gives the deck its signature slide. A **verifiable tool-only agent** answers a documented weakness: zero-shot Earth Engine LLM agents reached 40% accuracy, and their code failed to run more than 44% of the time ([ACL Findings 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.findings-acl.124/)). Digital Green's production system shows systematic accuracy limits outside training scope and uses atomic-fact evaluation against expert ground truth ([arXiv 2603.03294](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03294)). An agent that can only cite computed numbers differs visibly from BlinkFarms' Gemini chat. Two angles are first-mover bets with unverified feasibility. **NISAR** released a 200 m beta soil-moisture product in Feb 2026, but Delta coverage and flooded-rice behaviour are unknown ([Earthdata](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/over-100000-new-data-files-available-from-nisar)). **Seasonal-forecast regret minimization** has no rotation-specific prior work, but skill over Egypt was not checked, and in an irrigated system rainfall barely matters, leaving only temperature skill.

There is one trade-off to decide openly: NASA-native versus accurate. Precomputed 10 m embeddings beat Prithvi on cost and often on accuracy with few labels. TESSERA was about **28% more accurate** than the next method in a Senegal temporal-transfer test ([arXiv 2601.16900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16900v1)). Prithvi-EO-2.0 beats a supervised U-TAE on PASTIS by only about 0.5 mIoU ([arXiv 2412.02732](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.02732)). AlphaEarth is Google and TESSERA is Cambridge, though, so leading with them weakens "meaningful NASA data use". Resolution matters too. Delta holdings average about **1.6 feddan** ([ASU](https://research.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/221372)), and a 1-feddan plot is about 4.7 HLS 30 m pixels against about 42 Sentinel-2 10 m pixels. The resolution is to make Prithvi on HLS the official map, run TESSERA as an independent cross-check shown on one slide, and report results per 30 m block or per FTW polygon rather than per "field". FTW Global's 10 m boundaries are available but unvalidated for smallholders ([Source Cooperative](https://source.coop/ftw/global-data/llms.txt)).

## Recommended build: the Precrop Payoff Atlas

The pitch: *"Thousands of Delta farmers have already run the rotation experiment. NASA satellites recorded the results. We measure them, with honest error bars, and show each farmer the fields most like theirs."*

**Stage A, reconstruct history.** Pull HLS L30/S30 (2016–2025) for one or two districts in Kafr El-Sheikh/Gharbia. Gharbia overlaps the Tanta HLS phenology study, which used ground truth on tile 36RUV ([PMC7613390](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7613390/)). Fine-tune Prithvi-EO-2.0 (300M, or tiny as a fallback) for per-season classes: wheat/berseem/other in winter, rice/maize/cotton/other in summer. Labels come from the FAO WaPOR-2 Delta crop maps for 2023–24 ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/agroinformatics/training-and-resources/data-sets/data-set-detail/new-crop-type-mapping-data-for-egypt/en)), WorldCereal 2021 masks and reference points ([CEOS](https://ceos.org/gst/agriculture.html)), and photo-interpreted points. Published Delta benchmarks to compare against reach about **92.8% winter / 95.9% summer** OA on single-season Sentinel-2, and **0.77–0.80 in summer** at 30 m across multiple years ([ASU](https://research.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/217717); [MDPI Earth](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4834/6/3/80)). Check yearly rice area against national figures as a sanity check.

**Stage B, measure outcomes.** For each block-season, compute peak NDVI/GCVI from HLS and ET/LST from ECOSTRESS. Use SMAP and GPM as context. Add NASA POWER for heat stress, SRTM for elevation and distance to drains and the coast, and GRACE-FO as a Delta-wide storage trend only.

**Stage C, estimate effects.** Fit causal forests or DML with `grf`/`econml` for the effect of the precrop (berseem vs wheat) on next-crop vigor and ET. Covariates are weather, salinity proxies, elevation, distance to canal and drain, and governorate/rice-zone indicators. Read off heterogeneity by salinity and heat. Frame outcomes as "next-crop vigor and water use", never "soil health".

**Stage D, retrieve analogs and plan.** Embedding-nearest neighbours that switched become the visible evidence. A small GRU forecaster (the surviving piece of candidate 1) ranks at least 3 options including the status quo. It is constrained by Maas–Hoffman thresholds and rice-zone legality, and it penalizes options outside the data support.

**Stage E, explain.** An Egyptian-Arabic agent may only call these tools. Every sentence cites a computed number and its source, and a "challenge this" button reruns the estimate. For each option it prints the brief's five fields: why, evidence, benefits, gaps and risks.

### Validation judges can audit on one slide

Use held-out years and spatially blocked cross-validation by village or canal command, because crop choice clusters regionally. Run placebo tests: the "effect" of *next* season's crop on *last* season's vigor should be near zero, and permuting labels within strata should erase the effect. Add overlap diagnostics with propensity trimming to 0.05–0.95, following Kluger ([arXiv 2506.02384](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02384v2)), and omitted-variable sensitivity bounds, because fertilizer is unobserved. The external directional check is whether berseem comes out as the better precrop, as the Egyptian trials predict. Map error must be reported and propagated, since roughly 10% treatment misclassification attenuates effects. If the effect is null, say so; a measured null is still a finding. The forecaster must beat persistence and crop-climatology baselines out of location, or it gets dropped.

### Compute budget per Kaggle pipeline (estimates, not benchmarks)

| Pipeline | Main work | GPU need | Estimated wall-clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: HLS ingest + Prithvi fine-tune + inference | District clip, about 5–8 GB over 8 years; a few thousand labelled chips | T4, mixed precision | Download dominates; fine-tune about 1–3 h; inference minutes |
| B: Outcome features (HLS indices, ECOSTRESS, POWER, SMAP) | Per-block time series | CPU | 1–3 h, mostly I/O |
| C: Causal forest / DML + placebos | 10^4–10^5 block-seasons, tens of covariates | CPU | Minutes per model; under 1 h with the full robustness suite |
| D: Embeddings for analogs + GRU forecaster | Frozen Prithvi embeddings, small GRU | T4 | Under 1 h |
| E: Agent | Hosted or small LLM; tools precomputed | None at build time | Not applicable |

Prithvi-EO-2.0 ships an official multitemporal crop-segmentation notebook built for a T4 ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/ibm-nasa-geospatial/Prithvi-EO-2.0-300M)). Pretraining took about 21,000 GPU-hours, which is why only fine-tuning is in scope ([arXiv 2506.20174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20174)). The real risk is not GPU time. Two things were not verified: whether Kaggle can authenticate to Earthdata LP DAAC, and how fast it downloads. Stage HLS chips in a Kaggle dataset first.

### Pitfalls judges will raise, with the prepared answer

"Isn't the farmer's choice confounded?" Yes: rice follows the decree zones, salinity and canal position. The answers are zone-stratified overlap, placebo tests and sensitivity bounds, and the rice-zone boundary is itself a potential natural experiment. "Your 30 m pixels are bigger than the plots." Results are reported per block, with Sentinel-2-based TESSERA as a cross-check. "Is this NASA data or Google's?" NASA's HLS and Prithvi carry the core, and ECOSTRESS, SMAP, GPM, POWER, SRTM and GRACE-FO cover five of the brief's named datasets. "You can't see soil health from space." Agreed, and the Wallonia minimum-detectable-difference result is cited to show you know it. "Will the LLM hallucinate?" It cannot emit a number it did not compute. "Can farmers act on rice advice?" Only inside designated zones. Impact claims should stay modest. The first RCT of an AI farm advisor found changed practices but no short-run yield gain ([LinkedIn summary](https://tr.linkedin.com/in/ali-bakan-20579586); primary paper not retrieved), so promise better-evidenced decisions, not yield jumps. Finally, the presentation is only 7 slides or 30 seconds. Lead with one image: a Delta map where a farmer's block lights up its matched "switcher" analogs, each annotated with an effect and confidence interval.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "which AI is most impressive" to "which output is a measurement rather than a guess". Each AI-heavy candidate fails in the same place: it asks a model to predict effects the satellite record has never shown it, whether planned soil trajectories, carbon gains or salinity forecasts. The Delta's real asset is about a decade of farmer-run natural experiments over fields with documented agronomy, and no team in the 2026 field has measured them. Making that measurement the core turns Validity from the usual weak spot into the entry's main strength, while Prithvi, analog retrieval and a verifiable agent supply the state-of-the-art AI the brief asks for.

Three unknowns should be settled in the first hours of the event, because each can sink a pipeline: whether Kaggle can reach Earthdata, whether the FAO WaPOR Egypt labels can be downloaded and under what licence, and whether a district-level effect is detectable above map noise. If the effect turns out null, the honest pivot is to present the atlas as the Delta's first open rotation-history and evidence-gap map. That still answers the brief, and no other team has it.
