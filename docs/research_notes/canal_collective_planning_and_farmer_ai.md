# Canal-level collective crop planning in Egypt and the state of farmer-facing AI advisory (for Field Shift concept 5)

Research date: 2026-11-13. About 15 tool calls. Many findings come from search snippets and secondary summaries; the claims that carry the most weight are flagged so they can be checked. Older sources (2000s FAO/IIIMP) are marked as possibly out of date.

## 1. How Egypt's irrigation rotation and crop-planning institutions work in the 2020s

### Takeaway
Delta water is still largely delivered on rotation. Main feeders flow all the time, while branch-canal head regulators open in "on/off" turns (for example 5 days on and 10 off in a three-turn rotation). Mesqa-level Water User Associations exist but are uneven, and head-end/tail-end inequity is well documented. Compulsory collective crop rotation and delivery quotas were dismantled from 1987. The state still caps rice area by decree each year (724,200 feddans in 2025, with fines for planting outside it), zones cotton varieties by decree, and since 2016 has built a farmer smart-card and digital registry that is being matched with satellite land coordinates. So joint canal-level crop planning matches real constraints, but it brings back a coordination role the state formally dropped in 1987.

### Cited Findings
- Rotation mechanics (Mahmoudia canal area): main feeder canals flow continuously. Branch-canal head regulators open on a rotation schedule. Winter rotations were three-turn (5 days on, 10 off), and two-turn variants existed. During peak demand, tail-end farmers "may initially receive little or no water". (Early-2000s FAO/IIIMP document, possibly out of date.) — [FAO a0021e](https://www.fao.org/4/a0021e/a0021e09.htm); [FAO problem areas](https://www.fao.org/4/a0021e/a0021e06.htm)
- About 55.5 billion m3 of Egypt's irrigation water is delivered on a two-turn or three-turn rotation (older CIHEAM paper). — [CIHEAM](https://ressources.ciheam.org/ressources/om/pdf/b48/05002288.pdf)
- The Integrated Irrigation Improvement and Management Project (IIIMP, World Bank) aimed to move branch and mesqa levels from rotational to continuous flow, with mesqa improvement and farmer participation. Over 2,100 local WUAs were created to take over local O&M. — [FAO a0021e](https://www.fao.org/3/a0021e/a0021e09.htm); [2030 WRG institutional reform PDF](https://2030wrg.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/57-C-Institutional-reform-fo-irrigation-management.pdf)
- Mesqa-level WUAs were set up as part of institutional reform. — [WUR edepot](https://edepot.wur.nl/696030)
- A 2020 study in Kafr El-Dawar (Beheira) found that the irrigation rotation is irregular in practice. — [EKB journal article](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_87005_254ee564422489ad6f45ea88a28ade75.pdf)
- Water allocation to branch canals is "based on previous experience" rather than demand-driven (Ghanem & van Steenbergen). — [The Water Channel blog](https://thewaterchannel.tv/thewaterblog/on-water-allocation-in-large-irrigation-systems-the-case-of-egypt/)
- Al-Bayda secondary canal, western Delta (IWMI): farmers adapt by changing cropping patterns, crafting collective irrigation rules, reusing drainage water, and practicing deficit and night irrigation. More rice is grown where water is more available along the canal, and head-end over-irrigation reduces tail-end supply. Crop choice also reflects family food security, risk management, social capital, and history. — [CGIAR MEL repo](https://repo.mel.cgiar.org/handle/20.500.11766/6357); [IWMI "Heads or tails"](https://iwmi.org/news/heads-or-tails); [The Water Network](https://thewaternetwork.com/article-FfV/egyptian-farmers-adapting-to-water-scarcity-fJvITXPLZdtVcs0FRymEag)
- Liberalization: the mandatory area and delivery quota was abolished in 1987, replaced by optional delivery at guaranteed floor prices. Fixed procurement prices and marketing controls were removed from March 1987. FAO describes a shift "from mandatory crop rotation to farmer decision-based rotation". During 1987-1997 there was a lack of institutional support (extension, finance, R&D). — [FAO Y4632E](https://www.fao.org/3/Y4632E/y4632e0c.htm); [IFPRI/AgEcon RR115](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/16525/files/rr000115.pdf); [USAID](https://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/Pnack941.pdf)
- Rice decree: MWRI Ministerial Decree No. 26 of 2025 (Official Gazette, 3 Feb 2025) permits rice only in the listed governorates, up to a per-governorate area, 724,200 feddans in total. It sets water timing for nurseries and cultivation rotations from 15 May to 31 Aug, with earlier nursery water for Kafr El-Sheikh and Dakahlia. — [El Watan News (Arabic)](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/7820108)
- Earlier 2024 reporting said the area would be cut by about 32%, from about 1.1 million to about 750,000 feddans. This differs slightly from the final 724,200. — [Arab Finance](https://arabfinance.com/en/news/newdetails/18393); [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/business/energy/egypt-to-reduce-rice-cultivation-area-by-32-in-2025-over-water-shortage-iehjfjik)
- Under the 2021 water law, growing rice outside designated areas brings fines of EGP 3,000-10,000 per feddan or jail. Rice is restricted to nine Delta governorates (largest: Dakahlia, Kafr El-Sheikh, Sharqiya, Beheira, Gharbiya). — [USDA FAS](https://www.fas.usda.gov/data/egypt-egyptian-parliament-approves-prohibition-rice-cultivation-non-designated-areas); [World Grain](https://world-grain.com/articles/15360-egypt-implements-new-law-to-curb-illegal-rice-cultivation)
- Cotton: single-variety zoning since 1958/59. Every year a ministerial decree names the varieties allowed in each region, and each variety must be grown only in its zone. Lower-long-staple varieties are grown in the Delta and upper-long-staple in Upper Egypt. Law 4 of 2015 amended Law 210/1994 on cotton seed and production planning. Later amendments to the Agriculture Act gave the Agriculture Ministry, with the Irrigation Ministry, more power over which crops are grown where (report undated). — [Benha Univ thesis abstract](https://www.bu.edu.eg/portal/uploads/discussed_thesis/finalabsrtract/10781721.pdf); [Egyptian cotton traceability PDF](https://neu.baumwollboerse.de/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/EGYPTIAN-COCCTTON-TRACEABILITY.pdf); [USDA GAIN 2016](https://gain.fas.usda.gov/Recent%20GAIN%20Publications/Cotton%20and%20Products%20Annual_Cairo_Egypt_3-31-2016.pdf); [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/egypt/?p=629103)
- Infrastructure modernization: a national canal rehabilitation and lining program. JICA states the target was lining 20,000 km of secondary canals nationwide in 4 years (2020-2024). Officials give various completion figures (831 km, 900+ km, 1,760 km of an 8,239 km phase), which are not directly comparable. The stated aim is to "ensure delivery of water to the ends of canals". Modern on-farm irrigation is planned for about 4 million feddans of Old Lands. The World Bank FIMP piloted buried pipes in the Mahmoudia, Manaifa and Meet Yazid areas. — [JICA survey](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/1000052077.pdf); [Egyptian Gazette](https://egyptian-gazette.com/?p=7610); [ME Construction News](https://meconstructionnews.com/46228/egypt-completes-192m-rehabilitation-work-on-831km-long-canals-network/amp); [World Bank results brief 2020](https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2020/04/01/modernizing-irrigation-improved-water-security-for-farmers-in-egypt)
- A national project to register and digitize private irrigation canals (mesqas/private canals): phase one recorded more than 22,000 canals totaling over 17,000 km, rolling out across the Valley and Delta. — [Smart Water Magazine](https://smartwatermagazine.com/news/smart-water-magazine/egypt-advances-rollout-private-irrigation-canals-digitisation-project)
- Farmer smart card: introduced in 2016 and linked to Meeza payments through the Agricultural Bank of Egypt in 2020. It is now the only accepted payment method for fertilizer at cooperatives, with about 5,500 POS machines across 27 governorates, aimed at holdings under 25 feddans. The minister says satellite technology is being used to match land coordinates to smart cards. "Agri Egypt", an AI-labelled digital network with e-finance, is meant to be a complete database. An April 2026 MoU between ABE and eFinance plans a new Farmer Card with a pilot in late 2026. Sources name different ministers, so dates need checking. — [Amwal Al Ghad (fertilizer)](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=227554); [Amwal Al Ghad (tech-led farming)](https://en.amwalalghad.com/?p=223550); [Daily News Egypt (Agri Egypt)](https://www.dailynewsegypt.com/?p=763336); [Pulse (ABE/eFinance)](https://www.pulse.bot/fintech/news/agricultural-bank-of-egypt-teams-with-efinance-to-roll-out-digital-services-across-north-africa-fa30aeba-8ab2-4668-b9be-bf385083cc90/)
- IFPRI 2025: Egyptian digital ag services include apps, websites, call centers and SMS advisory (crop management, weather/climate alerts, prices). Only sensors and mobile apps are fairly widely used. — [IFPRI brief via CGSpace](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:fpr:prnote:178598)

### Inferences
- The canal-level problem is real. Water arrives in shared turns, rice is already rationed by area and by delivery window (15 May-31 Aug), and head/tail inequity drives crop choice (rice clusters near the head). A tool that plans crops per branch canal or mesqa so that rotation turns and the rice cap are respected answers real constraints.
- Politically, concept 5 is close to the pre-1987 state-coordinated rotation, which was abolished as coercive and inefficient. The pitch should present it as a voluntary decision-support and negotiation aid for WUAs or the Irrigation Advisory Service, not as compulsory consolidation.
- Plausible institutional hosts are WUAs and Branch Canal Water Boards, MWRI's private-canal digitization registry, and the smart-card/Agri Egypt registry, which matches plots to coordinates. These give a credible "who would use it" story.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 primary source on current nawba schedules (turn lengths per directorate) or on how far IIIMP's continuous-flow conversion has actually spread. The FAO description is from the early 2000s.
- The current status and effectiveness of the Irrigation Advisory Service (IAS) and Branch Canal Water Boards was not found.
- I did not confirm the term "dawra zira'iya" or the exact legal instrument that ended compulsory rotation; only "1987 abolition of mandatory area/delivery quotas" is sourced.
- I found no named Ministry of Agriculture farmer advisory app with usage figures.

## 2. Literature on landscape-level and collective crop allocation, ABMs, game theory, and LLM negotiation (novelty)

### Takeaway
Agent-based models of Egyptian irrigation exist (for example a 2026 Cornell ABM of Fayoum). So do classic non-LLM multi-agent irrigation negotiation models and LLM common-pool-resource simulations (GovSim). I found no published work that combines LLM farmer agents negotiating crop rotations with a hard canal-water constraint solver, for Egypt or anywhere else. The combination appears novel, but each component has precedent, and GovSim shows LLM agents are poor at sustaining shared resources unaided. That supports the design choice of making the solver enforce the constraint.

### Cited Findings
- Badawy (Cornell), EGU26-15647: an ABM of heterogeneous farmers in Fayoum. Pathway sets combine water sources, crop portfolios and infrastructure investments, constrained by wealth, farm size and canal position, under satisficing and bounded rationality. Finding: "farm size creates stratification in pathway diversity", far more than canal position. "Water infrastructure functions as resilience infrastructure by shaping who can adapt." No datasets are named in the abstract. — [EGU26-15647](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-15647.html)
- Lang & Ertsen: a general ABM of an irrigation system (farmer and canal agents), applied to ancient Mesopotamia. — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:87c9a3fe-aab9-4c5d-9631-aaa756c63aa5)
- Coupled farmer crop-choice agents with river and reservoir models, using positive mathematical programming calibration. — [EGUsphere 2023-1604](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2023/egusphere-2023-1604/egusphere-2023-1604-ATC1.pdf)
- Non-LLM multi-agent irrigation negotiation: Chiewchan et al. found that an auction approach gave higher profit for all farmers than direct negotiation at a fixed price. Barreteau et al. used agent-based facilitation of water allocation in the Drôme valley. — [Lincoln Univ archive](https://researcharchive.lincoln.ac.nz/entities/publication/c5b360c8-2c98-4ab9-b8cc-b1d2ac208485); [Typeset PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/agent-based-facilitation-of-water-allocation-case-study-in-1vq1t4zesf.pdf); [Simulating negotiations over limited water](https://www.conductual.com/articulos/Simulating%20negotiations%20over%20limited%20water%20resources.pdf)
- MARLIN (arXiv 2509.25034, AAMAS 2026): LLM-guided multi-agent RL for reservoir management. This is LLM guidance of RL, not negotiation. — [arXiv 2509.25034](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25034v3)
- Hosseini et al. (Water Research, 2026, as summarized in search): a perspective proposing LLM multi-agent systems for water engineering, including "negotiating agents to find a balanced solution considering differing goals". Conceptual, with no experiments. — [KWR library](https://library.kwrwater.nl/publication/110311)
- GovSim (Piatti et al., NeurIPS 2024): of 15 LLMs across fishery, pasture and pollution commons scenarios, only 2 of 45 runs sustained the resource. The best survival rate was below 54% (GPT-4o 53.3%). Failure is attributed to poor reasoning about how greedy actions affect long-run equilibrium. Communication reduced overuse by about 21%. The code is open-sourced. — [arXiv 2404.16698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16698)
- "Are LLMs Effective Negotiators?" is a general negotiation evaluation of LLMs, not water-specific. — [arXiv 2402.13550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13550)

### Inferences
- Novelty: high for the specific combination (Egypt Delta + mesqa/branch canal + LLM farmer agents + exact water-constraint solver + pest-adjacency term). Moderate for the components, since water-constrained crop planning by LP/MILP is decades old.
- Validity: GovSim is a strong citation for the hybrid design, because unconstrained LLM agents collapse commons, so a solver must enforce feasibility. Judges may still ask what the LLM adds over a pure MILP with farmer-preference weights. A defensible answer is that it elicits heterogeneous, qualitative preferences (food-security wheat, fodder for livestock, risk) and explains trade-offs in Egyptian Arabic. The IWMI al-Bayda finding that crop choice reflects food security, risk and social capital supports this.
- The Fayoum finding (farm size dominates canal position) cuts against putting canal position at the center. It is worth acknowledging, and fairness weighting by farm size could be included.

### Gaps
- I did not find peer-reviewed game-theory papers specific to Egyptian mesqa water sharing, though they likely exist in Water International or AWM. I also did not search MILP crop planning for the Delta specifically (e.g., El-Agha, Molden or Hassan papers).
- I did not find area-wide pest management evidence (cotton leafworm, pink bollworm, rice pests spreading between neighboring fields in Egypt) to back the pest-adjacency term with a citation. It needs a dedicated search.
- I did not verify any LLM-agent negotiation paper for agriculture or irrigation published in 2026.

## 3. Data availability: canal networks and command areas in the Delta

### Takeaway
No public, authoritative shapefile of Delta branch canals or mesqas with command-area boundaries turned up. Usable proxies are the new GRAIN global canal dataset (ESSD 2026, which names Egypt among the densest canal networks), OpenStreetMap waterways (Geofabrik extracts), and published reports describing intakes. MWRI's private-canal digitization (22,000+ canals) exists but is not public. Command areas would have to be approximated, for example as Voronoi or drainage-divide polygons around digitized canals over field boundaries derived from Sentinel-2 or HLS.

### Cited Findings
- GRAIN (Global Registry of Agricultural Irrigation Networks), ESSD 18:1855 (2026): an open dataset of more than 3.8 million km of irrigation canals in 95 countries. Egypt is among the countries with the densest canal networks relative to cropland. Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.16786488. Egypt resolution and format not checked. — [ESSD 2026](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/18/1855/2026/)
- OSM-derived Egypt shapefiles (Geofabrik) include waterways. Canal coverage in the Delta is unverified. — [GeoJamal OSM Egypt](https://maps.geojamal.com/2024/09/egypt-direct-download-of-free.html)
- CAPMAS governorate, Nile and lake shapefiles are hosted at Stanford/Berkeley but come with a restriction against reproduction or use without permission. — [Berkeley GeoData](https://geodata.lib.berkeley.edu/catalog/stanford-tp306xd1863)
- Eastern Nile Delta: about 1,880,000 feddans served by nine intakes (IWMI). The JICA report covers the Kased main canal command in the Central Delta. — [IWMI H048359](https://publications.iwmi.org/pdf/H048359.pdf); [JICA](https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/1000052077.pdf)
- MWRI private-canal digitization: more than 22,000 canals, over 17,000 km (no public download found). — [Smart Water Magazine](https://smartwatermagazine.com/news/smart-water-magazine/egypt-advances-rollout-private-irrigation-canals-digitisation-project)

### Inferences
- For a hackathon demo, take one well-studied branch canal (e.g., al-Bayda in the western Delta from IWMI, or a Mahmoudia or Meet Yazid command from FIMP). Trace canals from OSM or GRAIN, delineate fields from Sentinel-2 or HLS, and estimate per-field water demand from NASA data. That would be demonstrable and honest about its approximations.
- NASA data relevance (inference, not sourced here): HLS/Landsat for crop-type mapping and past rotations, OpenET/ECOSTRESS-type ET for crop water demand, SMAP soil moisture, GPM/IMERG precipitation, and MODIS/VIIRS for phenology. These would give concept 5 a strong "use of NASA data" score, because canal water budgets need ET-based demand.

### Gaps
- I did not check GRAIN's Egypt coverage, OSM canal completeness in the Delta, or whether nawba schedules per canal are published anywhere. Without real turn schedules, the water constraint has to use assumed values.

## 4. State of the art in farmer-facing AI advisory, including Arabic and Egyptian voice

### Takeaway
Farmer AI advisory is maturing quickly. Digital Green's Farmer.Chat reports reach in the hundreds of thousands to about 1 million users, and an IDinsight evaluation found 67% of active users applying advice. Rigorous impact evidence is still thin: the first RCT (Philippines and India rice) found better information access and changed practices but no short-run yield effect, and the Farmer.Chat Kenya RCT runs to March 2027. Hallucination is a recognized core problem. The SOTA response is expert-curated ground truth, atomic-fact evaluation, region-filtered RAG, and fine-tuning. Egyptian-Arabic farmer bots are prototypes only (a GIZ Agri Assistant, a vendor WhatsApp tomato-disease alert bot). Egyptian Arabic ASR still has roughly 18-40% WER depending on the model and test set.

### Cited Findings
- Farmer.Chat reach (figures from different dates and metrics conflict): 15,000 farmers and 300,000 queries (2024 paper). 125,000 users and 1.5M messages (Feb 2025, CGAP). India alone passed 1 million (10 lakh) users since its October 2024 launch (Rural Voice). Over 670,000 farmers in India, Kenya, Ethiopia and Nigeria (EC K4P). IDinsight third-party evaluation: 67% of active users applied advice. — [arXiv 2409.08916](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.08916.pdf); [CGAP](https://www.cgap.org/blog/beyond-chat-ai-powered-advice-for-women-farmers); [Rural Voice](https://eng.ruralvoice.in/digital-green-ai-farming-assistant-crosses-10-lakh-users); [EC Knowledge4Policy](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/node/85236_et)
- Digital Green (arXiv 2603.03294, Mar 2026): the production system shows "systematic accuracy limitations when models operate outside their training scope". The fix is LoRA fine-tuning on expert-curated facts plus a separate conversational delivery layer. The DG-Eval framework does atomic-fact verification (recall, precision, contradiction detection) against expert-curated ground truth. The authors released an expert QA dataset and a human preference dataset. They note that existing factuality evaluations (Wikipedia, web, retrieved context) do not fit safety-critical agronomy. — [arXiv 2603.03294](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03294)
- IFPRI-Digital Green Phase II (2025-2027) runs user testing in India and Kenya, reviewing technical accuracy, contextual relevance and fit with smallholder constraints. — [Rural21](https://www.rural21.com/english/news/detail/article/testing-ai-advisory-services-insights-from-farmerchat-in-india-and-kenya.html)
- AgriRegion (arXiv 2512.10114, Dec 2025): RAG restricted to verified local extension sources with geographic filtering. AgriRegion-Eval has 160 questions across 12 subfields. A secondary summary claims 10-20% fewer hallucinations than SOTA LLM systems (not verified against the paper). — [arXiv 2512.10114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10114v1)
- RCT: 1,935 rice farmers in the Philippines and India, with an AI advisor on WhatsApp or Messenger against a control chatbot without agronomic advice. Better information access and changed management, but no short-run effects on yields, costs or input adoption. Described by the authors as the first randomized evaluation of AI advisory. Source is a LinkedIn post summarizing the study, so the primary paper is unverified. — [LinkedIn post](https://tr.linkedin.com/in/ali-bakan-20579586)
- Farmer.Chat Kenya cluster RCT: 600 villages in Nakuru, measuring yields and income over two seasons, ongoing until March 2027. — [AEA RCT Registry 17035](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/17035)
- AIEP initiative (Kenya and Bihar, with cohorts including Gooey.ai): technical learnings paper. A 60 Decibels study of 800+ farmers found high satisfaction with availability and quality. The authors say rigorous yield and livelihood impact evaluations are still needed. — [arXiv 2601.11537](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11537v1); [BMZ Digital](https://www.bmz-digital.global/en/how-ai-is-transforming-farming-advice/)
- Farmerline Darli AI: voice and text in local languages, with WhatsApp. Over 6,000 smartphone farmers engaged on WhatsApp. — [GSMA](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/connectivity-for-good/mobile-for-development/mobile-for-development-2/agronomic-advisory-enhanced-by-ai-insights-from-farmerline/)
- OpenAI-linked farmer advisory in India reports yield increases (press coverage, not peer-reviewed). Kissan AI's Dhenu covers irrigation, pest and cultivation advice, with no evaluation found. Kissan-Dost (arXiv 2602.08593) is conversational IoT for smallholders. — [Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/deep-tech/openai-is-helping-farmers-in-india-increase-crop-yields); [arXiv 2602.08593](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.08593v1)
- Egypt-specific: the GIZ "Agri Assistant" prototype answers from an Egyptian agricultural database and warns that output may be inaccurate. A OneReach.ai vendor case study describes a WhatsApp early-warning bot for a tomato disease for Egyptian farmers. WideBot (Egypt) launched an Arabic multi-dialect AI voice agent for WhatsApp calls on its own Arabic LLM, not agriculture-specific. — [GIZ Agri Assistant](https://giz-agri-assistant.hf.space); [OneReach](https://onereach.ai/?p=11734); [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=18278)
- Egyptian Arabic ASR: a community Whisper-medium fine-tuned on about 72 hours of Egyptian speech self-reports 18.03% WER and 13.38% CER on its own validation split. Community Whisper-large-v3 Egyptian fine-tunes exist. In the NADI 2025 multi-dialect ASR shared task, the 2nd-place team (SeamlessM4T-v2 Large per dialect) averaged 38.54% WER, with Whisper large-v3 as a baseline. Talafha et al. 2023 evaluated Whisper zero-shot and fine-tuned on Arabic dialects. AraDiaWER argues WER poorly captures Egyptian ASR quality. SESTEK claims near-zero WER after Egyptian fine-tuning (vendor claim, no figures). — [HF whisper-medium-egy](https://huggingface.co/MAdel121/whisper-medium-egy); [HF whisper-large-v3-egyptian-arabic](https://huggingface.co/AbdelrahmanHassan/whisper-large-v3-egyptian-arabic); [NADI 2025 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.arabicnlp-sharedtasks.103.pdf); [Talafha et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.02902); [AraDiaWER](https://aclanthology.org/2023.fieldmatters-1.8.pdf); [SESTEK](https://www.sestek.com/blog/speech-recognition-accuracy-test-2024-arabic-edition)

### Inferences
- Delivery-layer best practice for any Field Shift concept:
  - Ground answers in a curated Egyptian source set (MALR/ARC extension bulletins, the rice decree, cotton zoning decrees) with region filtering, as in AgriRegion.
  - Evaluate on a small expert-checked "golden" QA set using atomic-fact precision and contradiction checks, as in DG-Eval.
  - Keep numbers (water quotas, dates, areas) computed by tools or solvers, not generated by the LLM.
  - Deliver on WhatsApp with voice notes, through a fine-tuned Whisper-large-v3 Egyptian model plus a confirm-back step, given 18-40% WER.
- Claims about impact in the pitch should be modest. The evidence supports better access and practice change, not proven yield gains.
- The Egyptian market gap is real: there is no scaled Arabic voice farmer advisory, only prototypes. That supports the Impact and Relevance framing.

### Gaps
- I found no Egyptian Arabic TTS quality benchmarks (e.g., MMS-TTS ara, XTTS, or commercial Egyptian voices) and no head-to-head Egyptian-only ASR benchmark (MMS vs Whisper large-v3 vs commercial).
- I found no peer-reviewed evaluation of Kissan AI or Gooey.AI specifically, nor CGIAR's own AI advisory accuracy numbers, nor any Google agronomy agent results (no AMIE-like agronomy agent found).
- The primary paper for the Philippines/India RCT was not retrieved.

## 5. Verdict: is concept 5 strong, defensible and novel for judges?

### Takeaway
Concept 5 is novel and grounded in real Egyptian constraints: rotation turns, the 724,200-feddan rice cap with delivery windows, head/tail inequity, mesqa WUAs, and an existing digital farmer registry. Its validity is weakest where data is missing: no public canal command areas or turn schedules, and unproven pest-adjacency effects. It is also vulnerable to the questions "why LLM negotiation instead of an optimizer?" and "isn't this the abolished pre-1987 forced rotation?". It works best as a constrained, demonstrable module (one branch canal, solver-enforced water budget from NASA ET, LLM used for preference elicitation and Arabic explanation) rather than as the headline full-system claim.

### Cited Findings
- Real constraint basis: rotation scheduling and tail-end shortage ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/a0021e/a0021e06.htm)), the rice area cap and delivery window ([El Watan](https://www.elwatannews.com/news/details/7820108)), crop clustering by canal position and collective irrigation rules ([IWMI](https://iwmi.org/news/heads-or-tails)).
- Risk that LLM agents degrade commons outcomes unaided: [GovSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16698).
- Policy history of mandatory rotation abolition: [FAO Y4632E](https://www.fao.org/3/Y4632E/y4632e0c.htm).
- Counter-evidence on canal position: [EGU26-15647](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-15647.html).

### Inferences
- Scoring by criterion:
  - **Impact**: high if framed for WUAs, MWRI or MALR rice and water planning.
  - **Creativity**: high.
  - **Validity**: medium. It is defensible with one-canal scope, a solver-enforced constraint, explicit assumptions, and an ET-based water budget.
  - **Relevance to NASA data**: high if water demand comes from ET, SMAP, GPM and HLS crop maps.
  - **Presentation**: strong visual potential (a canal map with an animated negotiation and a feasibility bar).
- Recommendation: use concept 5 as a differentiating feature layered on a simpler per-farm rotation advisor, not as the whole project. A hackathon team can deliver one canal and about 20 synthetic farmer agents. Present the LLM agents as "preference proxies that farmers confirm via WhatsApp", not as autonomous decision-makers.
